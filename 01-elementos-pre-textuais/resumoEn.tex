\begin{resumo}[Abstract]
The development of modern front-end applications demands ecosystems that balance delivery agility, maintainability, and code reliability. In this scenario, the debate between JavaScript (JS), characterized by dynamic typing, and TypeScript (TS), featuring an optional static typing system, has become central in software engineering. This Final Graduation Project investigates the perceptions and key factors influencing the adoption of JavaScript and TypeScript, comparatively analyzing differences between novice and experienced developers. The research employed a mixed-methods survey approach applied to 23 front-end developers, ranging from beginners to specialists with extensive experience in large-scale corporate projects. Data were processed using descriptive statistics and thematic categorization of open-ended qualitative responses. The findings demonstrate a notable perceptual divergence conditioned by professional seniority: while beginners prioritize JavaScript's flexibility and rapid prototyping capabilities (mean of 4.29 on a 5-point Likert scale), experienced professionals report substantial productivity gains with TypeScript (mean of 4.22 vs. 2.93 for beginners) and an overwhelming preference for adopting TypeScript in new projects (4.56). Both cohorts agree that the absence of static typing in JavaScript is prone to runtime errors (mean of 4.17) and that TypeScript substantially improves early bug detection (4.17 overall and 4.78 among seniors), long-term maintainability (4.44), and team code standardization (4.30). The study concludes that technology adoption transcends mere syntactic preference, representing a strategic architectural choice determined by project scale and team maturity.

\end{resumo}
